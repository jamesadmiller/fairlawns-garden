Catmint
H7 Fully Hardy● Drought: Excellent
↕ 60–90 cm↔ 60–90 cm☀ Sun / Part shadeFront · Middle

The classic cottage garden edging plant, and one of the most reliably drought-tolerant perennials there is. 'Andenken an Friedrich Hahn'
H4 Half-hardy◐ Drought: Good
↕ 60–90 cm↔ 30–45 cm☀ Sun / Part shadeMiddle

Deep wine-red tubular flowers carried in tall spikes from June through to the first hard frosts — one of the longest-flowering border plants available. A classic English country garden plant that complements Roses beautifully.

Lupinus polyphyllus

Russell Lupin
H6 Fully Hardy◐ Drought: Moderate
↕ 90–120 cm↔ 45–60 cm☀ Full sun / Part shadeMiddle · Rear

Lupins bring bold, spire-like flower columns of intense purple, pink and bicolour to the early summer border. The stately vertical habit provides strong architectural punctuation among softer cottage-garden plants. They prefer cool, moist springs but cope well with English conditions given good drainage and sun. Cut spent spikes hard back immediately after flowering to encourage a second flush. Excellent alongside Peonies, Geraniums and Roses — in late spring before those plants take over, Lupins are the undisputed showpiece.

Anemone × hybrida

Japanese Anemone
H5 Fully Hardy◐ Drought: Moderate
↕ 80–120 cm↔ 60–90 cm☀ Sun / Part shadeMiddle · Rear

Japanese Anemones fill the critical August–October gap when most perennials are fading, producing elegant saucer-shaped blooms in soft pink above dark green, vine-like foliage. They establish slowly but spread reliably once settled, forming impressive autumn clumps. Prefers positions with some moisture retention — avoid the very driest spots, which accounts for their occasional struggle in exceptionally hot summers. Outstanding at the front of Hydrangea and Magnolia where they naturalise beautifully over time.

Lavandula angustifolia

English Lavender
H5 Fully Hardy◈ Drought: Excellent
↕ 45–75 cm↔ 60–90 cm☀ Full sunFront · Middle

One of the most dependable plants in the English garden, and among the most drought-tolerant of all. 'Hidcote' is the benchmark — deep violet, very compact at around 45 cm, with an RHS AGM and near-universal availability. 'Munstead' flowers slightly earlier and is a little looser in habit. For more height and presence, *L.* × *intermedia* 'Grosso' (lavandin) reaches 75–90 cm with enormous spikes — excellent for cutting. Note: French lavender (*L. stoechas*, with the distinctive rabbit-ear bracts) is only H3 and may not survive a cold English winter reliably — treat it as a tender plant if you grow it.

Soil & placement

The Achillea, Eryngium, Echinops, Origanum and Nepeta will actively reward poor, stony conditions and tolerate dry spells. Geranium 'Rozanne' and Scabiosa cope well in partial shade cast by Hydrangea and Magnolia. Geum and Helenium prefer some moisture, so site them in the more sheltered positions.

Planting alongside the existing shrubs

The cool blue-violet tones of Geranium, Nepeta and Scabiosa soften Hydrangea and Rhododendron without competing. The warm orange and wine tones — Achillea, Helenium, Penstemon 'Garnet' — are best grouped near Roses. Verbena bonariensis works as a see-through plant throughout, particularly in front of taller shrubs.

Half-hardy notes

Verbena bonariensis (H3) and Penstemon 'Garnet' (H4) are both worth treating as you do the tender Salvias — take insurance cuttings in August using the semi-ripe method in the propagation guide. Both will often come through a UK winter unscathed, but the cuttings guarantee continuity.
